# What Seattle and Portland Can Learn From Each Other (July 2026)

Notes from research prompted by a Seattle visit — the downtown/waterfront revival there vs. Portland's recovery, and what transfers. Companion docs in this folder: vacancy inventory, bridge research, civic-stack architecture, momentum plan.

## Where each city stands

**Seattle:** Downtown visitors hit 98% of June 2019 levels in June 2026 (3.2M unique visitors); downtown residents up ~21% vs. 2019 (63,000+ occupied apartments); hotel demand at 109% of 2019. Office workers still lag at 64% of pre-pandemic. The new Waterfront Park logged 3.2M visits in its first season (May–Sept 2025), with 309 public activations and $2.5M in vendor revenue. (Sources: DSA dashboard; KOMO.)

**Portland:** Central city foot traffic ~86% of pre-pandemic — mid-pack among peers. Office vacancy at an all-time high of 10.2M sq ft; 2025 leasing the lowest on record outside 2020. Metro Chamber's 2026 State of Downtown: recovery cannot depend on office demand returning; visitor activity is the biggest gap and opportunity. Residential foot traffic is the relative bright spot.

## What Seattle is doing that Portland can learn from
1. **A conservancy + dedicated funding, not just a park.** Friends of Waterfront Park runs programming (72 free festivals, 202 artist/org partnerships, 82% BIPOC-led), hospitality/safety staff, vendors. Capital partly from a ~$160M Local Improvement District on benefiting downtown properties. The park is an institution, not a space.
2. **Connectivity as the unlock.** Overlook Walk (Oct 2024) stitched Pike Place to the waterfront — 611,000 visits in season one. Activation follows connection.
3. **Mayor-level singular focus.** Harrell's Downtown Activation Plan: vacant storefront activation (Seattle Restored), street-activation code changes, office-to-residential program, Third Ave transit investment. Criticized as slow (The Urbanist: "stuck in low gear"), but the sustained focus + public dashboard matter.
4. **Downtown as a neighborhood.** Resident growth (+21% vs 2019) carries downtown while office workers stay hybrid.
5. **Event anchors + visitor economy.** World Cup, waterfront festivals; 61% of waterfront visitors were WA residents from outside Seattle.

## What Portland has that Seattle can learn from
1. **Housing/zoning reform leadership.** Residential Infill Project (2020) — Sightline: best low-density zoning reform in US history; Oregon HB 2001 statewide; Washington followed years later.
2. **Regional governance.** Metro — the only directly elected regional government in the US — plus the urban growth boundary.
3. **Shelter-first pragmatism.** Mayor Wilson's overnight-shelter push to end unsheltered homelessness; data contested with the county but nationally watched.
4. **Small-scale urbanism.** Food carts, cheap retail formats, neighborhood main streets, bike infrastructure.

## Activating Portland's downtown + waterfront
- Timeline realism: Seattle's waterfront took ~20 years because a highway had to come down. Portland removed Harbor Drive in 1974 — Tom McCall Waterfront Park exists; this is a retrofit. The design competition (Portland Parks + Parks Foundation, Metro grant) for a "toes-in-the-water" riverfront is underway.
- Borrow Seattle's model: pair the McCall retrofit with a funded conservancy + LID-style financing + year-one programming cadence; don't wait for construction to activate.
- Office-to-residential + Vacant Spaces Project; office demand isn't coming back (Chamber).
- OMSI district + Zidell Yards = second waterfront anchor (no Seattle equivalent).
- Visitor economy is the biggest measured gap: signature events, hotel drivers, PSU as anchor.

## Beyond the waterfront: what's moving downtown (mid-2026)
- **Darcelle XV Plaza** opened 6/18/26 — $9.2M rebuild of O'Bryant Square; stage, dog run, programmable lighting; SDCs + $2.2M general fund.
- **Portland Diamond Project / MLB at Zidell Yards** — purchase agreement; unanimous council support (10/2024); Manfred confirms expansion coming.
- **Keller Auditorium** renovate-vs-replace process.
- **Central City Task Force → Wilson's Central City Roundtable** (3/2026) — "All In" roadmap.
- **Central City Vacant Spaces Project** + Clean & Safe retail grants.
- **Green Loop** — serious planning, sounding boards through 2026; Friends of Green Loop.
- **Arts tax reform** at council (5/2026).
- **James Beard Public Market** — Selling Building at SW 6th & Alder, <1 block from Pioneer Courthouse Square; ~38,000 sq ft, 3 floors + rooftop, ~40 businesses; phased opening into fall 2026; designs approved 5/2026.

## The West Burnside / Providence Park problem
Paradox: 25,000 on match days (~$187M soccer economy), Powell's + Crystal Ballroom on the corridor, MAX adjacent — yet it struggles. Why: (1) **orphaned seam** — N/S address divide, edge of five neighborhoods, center of none; (2) **stroad** — High Crash Corridor, pedestrian crashes ~3× citywide; PBOT fixes piecemeal while the Burnside–Couch couplet concept sat shelved ~20 years; (3) **surge economics** — ~40 event days can't sustain retail without a weekday base.

What could work: one bridge-to-stadium corridor plan; stadium-district strategy (Wrigleyville model — Timbers GNA table, match-day street closures); enhanced service district west of 10th or an 82nd-Ave-style corridor coalition; density around the three MAX stations + West End conversions; care-based street management (day spaces, shelter network, deflection, Good Neighbor Agreements with the missions).

### Burnside–Couch couplet status
1960s proposal (bridge → NW 23rd; vestige: spare Couch overpass at I-405). 2007 concept plan adopted; east half built (early 2010s); west half never funded (recession + Pearl opposition). Realistic western terminus was ~NW 15th/16th. No active project; planning fashion has moved against one-way couplets. The live opportunity is the EQRB closure window — now postponed indefinitely (see bridge research); delay = prep years.

### Who's working on it
Better Block PSU + Downtown/Pearl/Old Town NAs ("Bridgeless to Better Burnside," June 2025, commissioned by Better Block PDX). Champions: Stickler, Sweat, O'Meara; Hashagen leads Better Block PDX. No banner/name/website; stadium end unrecruited (GHFL, NWDA, Friends of Couch Park, Timbers, MAC, Powell's).

## How citizens get active
District councils (4×3, post-2024 charter reform) — testify, serve on boards/commissions (portland.gov/civic/get-involved). Neighborhood associations (PDNA, Old Town CA, GHFL, NWDA). Advocacy: Portland: Neighbors Welcome, Strong Towns PDX, The Street Trust, Friends of Green Loop, Friends of Couch Park. Waterfront: Portland Parks Foundation, Human Access Project. Hands-on: SOLVE, Clean & Safe events. And show-up economics: with foot traffic at 86%, being a downtown regular is a measurable act.
