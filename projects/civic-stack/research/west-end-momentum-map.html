Better Burnside · research memo · July 2026

THE POOL, THE SEAM,
AND THE CLUSTER.

Since 2023, public money and private operators have built a pool of eating-drinking-gathering momentum along SW Alder and Harvey Milk between 5th and 9th — the Midtown Beer Garden, Flock Food Hall, Darcelle XV Plaza, and this fall the James Beard Public Market. All of it sits a 3-to-6-block walk south of W Burnside. The demand is real and it is climbing toward the street. Burnside is the seam where it stops — and the stadium-block vacancy cluster is the far shore.

The pool · 5th–9th, Alder–Harvey Milk

Demand is proven, three blocks south

The oldest cart pod in the city reborn as a 300-seat beer garden (2023). An 8,000 SF food hall on the old Alder cart block, built with a $3M Prosper Portland loan (2025). A rebuilt civic plaza with a stage (June 2026). A 40-stall public market opening phased (fall 2026). Four bets, one walkable pocket — and every one had public capital or public land behind it.

The seam · W Burnside

Five lanes nobody crosses for fun

The pool's northern edge is Burnside. Powell's and the Crystal Ballroom prove people will come to the street itself — but between them and the stadium the frontage goes dark. A High Crash Corridor with 4 deaths in a decade doesn't invite the pool to spill north; it walls it off. PBOT is repaving 22 blocks of it as-is right now.

The cluster · 15th–20th

The far shore, going dark

Nearly every storefront on the stadium blocks is vacant, closing, or at risk — while 25,000 match-day fans walk past. This is exactly the Phase 1 repave segment. And the proof it can work is already on the block: Mox Boarding House (1938 W Burnside) has thrived here since 2020. The pool shows what fills the rest: carts, halls, beer gardens, plazas — thousands-of-dollars moves, with a city loan program that has already funded two of them.

The pool is moving toward the street

Each opening lands closer to the West End's north edge. The unanswered question is whether the next one crosses Burnside — or whether the corridor keeps exporting its demand south.

  • Aug 2023Midtown Beer GardenSW 5th & Harvey Milk · 3 blocks off Burnside

    The city's oldest food-cart pod (est. early 1980s) renovated into a 20-cart, 300-covered-seat beer garden with a Fracture Brewing bar and a stage.

  • Feb 2025Flock Food Hall917 SW Alder @ 9th · 6 blocks off Burnside

    8,000 SF, nine vendors, in Block 216 beside the Ritz-Carlton — on the block the famous Alder Street carts once held. $3M Prosper Portland loan.

  • Jun 2026Darcelle XV Plaza800 SW Harvey Milk @ Park · 3 blocks off Burnside

    Former O'Bryant Square rebuilt: stage, event lawn, dog run. Ribbon-cut June 18, fully open June 25. Already on the Better Burnside parks string — it's the hinge: Park Blocks run straight north across Burnside.

  • Fall 2026James Beard Public MarketSW 6th & Alder (Selling Building) · 4 blocks off Burnside

    ~40,000 SF, 40 vendor stalls, teaching kitchen. Phased opening. The pool's biggest bet yet.

  • Next?The corridor's move

    Aim the same playbook — meanwhile-leases, cart pods, Prosper-style capital — at the stadium cluster, and program the parks string so the pool has a reason to walk north. That's Moves 2, 3 and 4 of the campaign plan.

What this means for the campaign

Three uses, no scope creep: it's evidence (demand exists at Burnside's doorstep — the corridor isn't failing for lack of it), it's a playbook (cheap activation + public co-investment fills space in this exact market), and it's a recruiting list (operators and funders who've already done it six blocks away). It does not become a new tracked vector on the site.
