# Better Burnside — ideas

- **The Bridgehead Rain Pavilion** (GZ, 7/21/26) — **full research doc:
  civic-stack/research/2026-07-21-bridgehead-rain-pavilion.md; now a second PILOT PROPOSAL
  on the draft site (7/21). CLOCK: McCall design competition launched 7/8/26, winner spring
  2027 — engagement via Interplay is the channel. (Director Park fireplace claim dropped —
  unverified; the glass canopy is the precedent.)** Original sketch: mass-timber covered playground + beer garden
  + fire pits at Waterfront Park's north end — the underused block just south of Saturday
  Market, between Ankeny and Ash (not part of the main festival zone). Designed to flourish in
  summer AND rain: operable louvered roof for good weather; in bad weather the rain becomes the
  attraction — clear walls with rain sheeting through them, backlit; fires going; warm timber
  overhead; kids dry under the canopy. Rain-city counter-programming: the only park in Portland
  that gets BETTER in November. Why it fits the campaign: it's the corridor's EAST anchor —
  bookends Providence Park at the west end; first/last stop on the crossings trail ("the river
  & the bridge" terminus points at it); Saturday Market delivers 100Ks of visitors to its edge
  already; and when the bridge closure finally comes, the bridgehead needs a destination or it's
  a dead end — this is the "pilot now, scale in the closure" move for the east end. Portland
  proof points: PDX airport's beloved mass-timber roof (the material's hometown flex), Director
  Park's canopy + outdoor gas fireplace (mini precedent — verify current operation), the covered-
  playground gap PNW parents complain about every winter. Partners/vehicles: **McCall Waterfront
  design competition (underway — the submission vehicle)**, Portland Parks Foundation, Oregon
  Mass Timber Coalition / TallWood Design Institute (timber industry loves a showcase),
  Saturday Market, Parks-on-Tap-style operator (see parks-vending-precedents.md — beer legal
  as events today; rotating-garden policy is ask #2), Travel Portland. Ties directly to the
  waterfront-conservancy gap: this is the kind of revenue-generating anchor (beer garden +
  concessions, NYC model) that makes a conservancy fundable. Status: champions-menu idea;
  waterfront-WIDE ambitions stay parked (no owner), but this bridgehead block is corridor work.

- **Themed crossings trail** (GZ, 7/21/26) — **now a "PILOT PROPOSAL" section on the draft
  site** (in The Plan, 7/21/26): 7 crossings, themes point where the cross-street leads,
  partner targets in hover tooltips, Cat-Trail precedent figures + PBOT permit path cited.
  Original sketch: public-art crossing zones at key intersections,
  each themed to what that cross-street leads to — Rose Garden/Washington Park at the west end,
  Timbers at 18th–20th, Laika (Coraline/Wildwood→Forest Park) at the NW gateways, Darcelle/
  Pride at the Triangle (12th–13th), Powell's/books at 10th, Gorge/Multnomah Falls, bridge/
  Skidmore at the east end. Art on both sides + themed intersection treatment; each crossing
  sponsored (adjacent institution: Powell's, McMenamins, MAC, Timbers) or community-supported
  (NA adopts one). "Explore them all" = a bridge-to-stadium walking trail — the string of
  pearls made legible; tourist content for Rose Light. Mechanics: PBOT Portland in the Streets
  street-painting permits (intersection murals — note FHWA/PBOT generally bar art *inside* the
  marked crosswalk bars, so design as crossing-zone murals + curb art); Bloomberg Asphalt Art
  Initiative playbook + its safety stats tie art to the High Crash Corridor case. Timing hook:
  Phase 1 repave = fresh blank asphalt on exactly the cluster blocks. Fits plan moves 2/3/5;
  cheap, sponsorable, each crossing recruits its own coalition member. Champions-menu item —
  not on the site until the coalition picks it up. (Verify: current PBOT street-painting
  permit terms; Asphalt Art crash-stat citation.)
  **Laika appetite: PROVEN, and the window is NOW (verified 7/21/26).** Coraline's Curious Cat
  Trail (Aug–Oct 2024): 31 statues downtown w/ Clean & Safe + Visit Downtown + Wild in Art —
  135,000 visitors, ~$4M downtown economic impact, $324.5K auction for Doernbecher.
  **Wildwood: Follow the Crows launches early Aug 2026** (31 crows, thru 10/31/26, same
  partners/auction). CORRECTED 7/21: cats DID hit Crystal/Pride Plaza, Saturday Market, Rose
  Garden — as scattered stops, not a route. Ask: make the corridor itself a trail route —
  Powell's→Mox→Providence Park segment; channel is Clean & Safe
  (already a coalition target) / Wild in Art artist-call process. The cats' $4M impact number
  is the sponsor pitch for themed crossings. Sources: businesswire.com/news/home/20241108160316,
  wildinart.co.uk/events/wildwood-follow-the-crows, kgw.com (crows trail), kptv.com 3/5/26.
  **West terminus upgrade (GZ, 7/21/26):** the corridor's real end is the Washington Park
  entrance just past NW 23rd — under-celebrated today. Trail terminus = a lit archway + lit
  sign over the entrance ("the corridor's front gate to the forest"). On the draft site 7/21.
